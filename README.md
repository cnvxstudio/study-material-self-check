# Source-checked study resources

A short, curated collection for studying from a passage, checking an answer against its source, and keeping track of what needs another review.

## Learning methods and recall practice

- [The Learning Scientists: downloadable materials](https://www.learningscientists.org/downloadable-materials) — Posters, slides and prompts covering retrieval practice, spaced practice and other learning strategies. Follow the linked resources' own attribution and reuse terms.
- [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) — Flashcards with scheduled reviews for revisiting difficult material. The desktop application is free; the official iOS application is a separate paid product. Useful after you have checked a card's answer against the original passage.

## Keep the source within reach

- [Zotero](https://www.zotero.org/) — Collect, organize, annotate and cite research sources. Keep the document and its bibliographic details together before turning a claim into a practice question.
- [PDF.js](https://mozilla.github.io/pdf.js/) — Mozilla's open-source PDF parsing and rendering project. A resource for developers building a document viewer alongside a study workflow.

## A document-based study routine

- [Study material self-check guide](https://github.com/cnvxstudio/study-material-self-check/blob/main/SKILL.md) — A reusable workflow with a worked example: select a bounded passage, prepare questions, compare the answer with the source, and revisit missed concepts. It can be followed manually or by an assistant.
- [AI Study Tools](https://aistudytools.app/) — Our document-based practice tool. Upload text-based PDF, DOCX, TXT or Markdown material, or paste notes; select a scope and generate practice questions or a study guide. Explanations and source references support checking against the material. At the time this list was prepared, an account included one free generation shared between exams and guides; saved content could be revisited. Maintained by CNVX, which also maintains this list.

## How to choose

1. Start with one chapter or a few related paragraphs.
2. Use the learning-method resources to choose a revision routine.
3. Keep a route back to the original passage with a source organizer or your document viewer.
4. Practise recalling an answer, then compare its wording and conditions with the source.
5. Record the concept you missed and return to it in a later session.
