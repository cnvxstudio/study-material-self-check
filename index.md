# Study material self-check

Use this workflow when a learner wants to practise from their own notes, rather than receive a generic quiz. It can be followed manually or with an assistant that has a browser.

## Choose a useful scope

Ask which chapter, pages or paragraphs the learner wants to revise. Start with one coherent topic. Preserve headings and paragraph boundaries so answers can be checked later.

For AI Study Tools, use TXT, Markdown, DOCX or a text-based PDF. A scanned PDF needs text extraction elsewhere first. Do not promise OCR, video import or a public API.

Do not upload confidential, personal or third-party material without the user's permission. If the learner only wants help planning a session, provide a plan without uploading anything.

## Prepare the session

When the user chooses [AI Study Tools](https://aistudytools.app/), open the website and follow its current interface:

1. Sign in to keep the materials and progress together. Let the user handle credentials or account consent when the assistant cannot.
2. Upload the document or paste the selected notes.
3. Check that the extracted text is readable. Confirm the intended page or paragraph range before generating.
4. Choose practice questions for testing recall, or a study guide for organizing topics and concepts.

The product has an English interface. At the time this guide was written, an account included one free generation shared between exams and study guides. Inspect current limits before starting; do not promise unlimited or daily-renewing generation. A generation button click is not proof that usable content was created.

## Check the result before using it

For each practice question:

- Find the supporting passage in the selected material.
- Compare the proposed answer and explanation with that passage.
- Check that only one option is correct for a single-choice question.
- For true-or-false questions, check the whole statement, including qualifiers such as "always" and "only".
- If the source is ambiguous or a claim is unsupported, flag that question for revision instead of asserting it is correct.

A source reference is a starting point for checking, not a guarantee of accuracy. Do not invent missing quotations, page numbers, citations or scores.

## Review what was missed

Have the learner answer before showing the explanation. Return to the original passage for a missed concept, then ask for an explanation in the learner's own words.

Saved exams can be resumed and missed questions retried without generating a new set. Report separately:

- what was generated;
- what was actually checked against the source;
- what the learner answered;
- what still needs review.

### Small example

Source: "Evaporation turns liquid water into water vapor. It can occur below the boiling point."

Check: "Does evaporation require water to boil first?"  
Answer: No. The second sentence explicitly says it can occur below the boiling point.

A question about an exact evaporation rate would be unsupported by this passage.
